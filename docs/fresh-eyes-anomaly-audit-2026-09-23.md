# Fresh-Eyes Anomaly Audit — 2026 Mayflower Quarter Photo Set
**Date:** September 23, 2026
**Method:** Every image in the project photo folder was visually inspected, one file at a time, by independent examiners working only from the pixels. No earlier conclusions were reused — every verdict below was re-derived from the images themselves.

## 1. Exact coverage

- **548 image files examined, each exactly once:** 69 top-level study photos + 479 photos in `first500/`.
- **18 video files:** 4 frames extracted from each (at 20%, 40%, 60%, and 80% of running time) with ffmpeg — **72 frames examined**.
- Nothing was skipped. Files that would not open are listed in section 9.

A quick plain-English note on what "examined" means here: each photo was opened and looked at the way a coin-error specialist looks at a coin — checking every raised line, blob, blur, and scratch, noting exactly where it sits on the coin (using clock positions, like "a bump at 3 o'clock next to the sail"), and judging whether it came from the die (the metal stamp that strikes the coin), from handling after minting, or from the photography itself.

### Short jargon guide (used in this report)
- **Die chip:** a small raised blob on the coin, caused when a tiny piece of the steel die breaks out. The coin metal flows into the gap and leaves a lump.
- **Die crack:** a thin raised line on the coin, caused when the die itself cracks. Coin metal squeezes into the crack and leaves a ridge.
- **Die break / cud:** a larger version — a bigger broken piece of the die leaves a bigger distortion, sometimes involving the rim.
- **Doubled die:** the die itself was made with a doubled image, so every coin it strikes shows doubling. Different from **machine doubling**, which is a small flat shelf-like doubling caused by the coin shifting slightly during the strike — common and minor.
- **Strike-through:** something (grease, cloth, debris) got between the die and the coin blank during striking, leaving a weak or odd patch.
- **Contact mark / bag mark:** scratches, nicks, and dings the coin picked up after it was made — from other coins, counting machines, or fingers. Not a mint error.
- **Planchet flaw:** a defect in the blank metal disc before it was struck.
- **Die polish lines:** fine parallel lines left when the mint polishes a die; they transfer faintly to coins. Usually harmless.
- **Mint mark:** the tiny letter showing which mint made the coin — **P** for Philadelphia, **D** for Denver.
- **Working die:** one individual steel stamp. A working die strikes thousands of coins, and all of them can share that die's cracks and chips. Finding which photos came from the same working die is the core of this audit.

## 2. Headline verdict

**Zero coins in the entire set meet the Phantom of Larose rule.** Not one coin carries all five required markers at the same time.

Out of David's seven markers, exactly **one** was positively confirmed, on **one** physical coin:

- **Mosquito Bite — CONFIRMED once.** A raised bump on the lady pilgrim's left arm, photographed in `2025-04-10_phantom-larose-008/009/010` (009 and 010 are the same coin shot 13 seconds apart — the bump appears in both, proving it is really on the coin and not a speck of dust or a photo glitch). This is a genuine small die chip, medium confidence.

Everything else:

- **Splinter (thin sliver on the left pinky) — not found on any coin.** The close-up hand photos show only round blobs and dots — never a thin sliver. The sharpest macro of the pinky area (first500-360, Denver mint) shows a normal pinky.
- **Ant Bite — not found on any coin.**
- **Elbow anomaly — one medium-confidence candidate.** The coin labeled "D10 A-1" (first500-456/457/458/459 — same coin photographed four times, plus its reverse 455) shows a repeated lump on the lady's left elbow that reads as a possible die chip, medium confidence. One other coin (first500-477) shows a smaller bump at the elbow, low-to-medium confidence. Neither is confirmed; both deserve a sharper re-photo before being called a marker.
- **Blurry letters/face — not found.** Wherever lettering and faces are sharp enough to judge, they are crisp — the opposite of the marker. No doubling of the doubled-die type was confirmed anywhere (one possible slight doubling on "RS" initials in first500-367, low confidence; one possible doubling along a hand edge in the 2026-09-13 series, low-to-medium on the type).
- **Anchor — not found anywhere.** No anchor-shaped mark appears on any coin in the set.
- **Phantom stages (duck head → half phantom → full Phantom at the front of the main mast) — none observed.** Every reverse where the mast-front area is visible shows rigging, sails, and field — no bird-like figure at any stage. Many reverses are too soft to fully resolve the area (honestly recorded as "unclear" rather than "absent"), but zero show any phantom form.

## 3. Genuine die events found (the honest positives)

The audit was not empty-handed. These are real, defensible mint-error findings, each with its confidence level:

1. **Obverse die-crack network through the date — HIGH confidence.** Files first500-076 through 096 document a raised crack running through "1776" (076–087, including a wide crack band in 086–087), continuing through "~2026" and down from the rim through the "T" of TRUST (088–096), plus a separate rim-to-shoulder field crack near the "RS" initials (065–067). These are sharp, clearly raised lines — textbook die cracks. This is the strongest die-event cluster in the whole collection.
2. **Reverse branching die crack below the "U" of UNITED — medium-high confidence.** `2026-09-17_coin3-crack-map-red-lines.jpg` and `2026-09-17_coin3-field-crack-mast-top.jpg` are the same coin photographed twice; the jagged branching raised crack appears in both. (The straight red line drawn on one of them is the photographer's annotation, not part of the coin.)
3. **Reverse raised crack line in the field — medium-high confidence.** `2026-05-04_phantom-larose-014_reverse-crack-closeup.jpg`.
4. **Recurring raised curved arc below the bow on Denver reverses — medium to medium-high.** Fifteen D-mint reverses (takeout-032, 035–039, 041–044, 046–048, 050, 052; clearest in 039) share the same curved raised line sweeping through the field below the bow toward the rim. Either one Denver working die carrying a die scratch, or one physical coin re-photographed across sessions from August 25 to September 13. Kept as one cluster (see section 5).
5. **Die chips, medium confidence:** a raised elongated blob at the stern/hull spar junction (takeout-045/055 — sequential photos, same coin); blobbing at the bowsprit tip (batch-01 mast-top photo; first500-463, 479, 497); small chips on the clasped-hand/wrist edges (2026-09-13 hand series); a blob on the "U" of UNITED (takeout-047).
6. **Probable die crack across the male pilgrim's face — medium-high.** Video first500-064.mp4, frame at 20%: a raised diagonal hairline running roughly 11 o'clock to 4 o'clock across the face.
7. **Jagged raised line through obverse drapery folds — medium.** Microscope shots first500-467/468 (same area, two framings).
8. **Possible small die chips near the date tilde — low.** first500-361/362 and 364/365 (two coins, tiny raised marks by the "~" in "1776~2026"); first500-366 (faint straight line through "1776," possible die crack, low-to-medium).
9. **Possible rim cud vs. rim damage — low-to-medium, needs better photos.** first500-147/148 show a rough lumpy rim at 12–2 o'clock; the current photos cannot decide between a small rim die break and post-mint damage.

A note on confidence: "medium" here means the feature is really on the coin but its exact cause is debatable; "low" means it might be a trick of light, a scratch, or a photo artifact. Nothing in the low-confidence tier should be presented as a proven error without a sharper re-photo.

## 5. Evidence that cuts against the working theory

Reported straight, as requested:

1. **No coin carries all five markers.** The rule David set — all five or it isn't the Phantom — fails on every coin in a 548-photo set. The closest any coin gets is one marker out of five (the Mosquito Bite coin).
2. **The Splinter does not appear.** The single most specific marker — a thin sliver of displaced metal on the left pinky — was not seen on any coin, including the sharpest pinky macro in the set. Only round dots and blobs appear, which are a different thing (a sliver is a line; a blob is a dot).
3. **No phantom figure at the mast.** No duck head, no half phantom, no full Phantom on any reverse. The "phantom zone" at the front of the main mast shows ordinary rigging wherever it is visible.
4. **No anchor.** Zero anchor-shaped marks in the entire set.
5. **Lettering and faces are crisp, not blurry.** The blurry-letters/face marker fails everywhere it can be tested — the opposite condition is what's actually in the photos.
6. **Some arrowed "markers" are ordinary design.** On close inspection, several features the photo annotations point to as markers resolve as normal design relief: knuckle and fingertip dots on the hand, drapery folds, and the "PH" designer initials on the reverse. This means some markers were being read into the normal design, not into anomalies.
7. **The "500" is far fewer than 500 coins.** Heavy re-photography runs through the whole set — workers estimate the 479 first500 files collapse to roughly on the order of 150–250 distinct specimens at most (for example, batch-06's 44 files hold ~19–20 distinct coins; batch-12's 43 hold ~22). Conclusions about "many coins" should be restated as "many photos of fewer coins."
8. **The folder is a mixed camera roll, not a pure audit set.** It contains a 1944 Mercury dime (15 photos), a 1976 Bicentennial quarter (4 photos), worn Barber coins, microscope-screen photos, app screenshots (Die Clash Analyzer, TikTok, Gemini, Facebook), iPhone lock screens, 13 Louisiana OMV screenshots, blueprints, a packing slip, QR codes, a marketing graphic, and two videos of a truck and a van. Non-Mayflower material was excluded from all die-grouping.
9. **The crack-template composites mix coins.** The red-line templates combine crack observations from multiple different coins onto one drawing. They are useful as a checklist of "things seen somewhere," but they do not show that all those cracks exist on one coin or one die.
10. **The single confirmed marker is a single coin.** The Mosquito Bite bump is real but appears on one physical coin — a lone die chip, not a progression across stages or dies.

None of this rules out that a Phantom of Larose coin could exist somewhere unphotographed. It does mean **this photo set contains no Phantom of Larose** under David's own rule, and no photographic evidence of the duck-head → half → full progression.

## 6. Photo-set integrity notes

- **Corrupted files (4):** first500-172, 173, 174 and first500-391 render as full-frame rainbow line noise — file corruption or glitched captures. If these were meant to be coin shots, they need re-capturing.
- **Unassessable files (4):** `2026-09-04_takeout-017` (blurry screen photo), first500-272, 273 (extreme blur), 276 (pixelated microscope-screen photo).
- **Mirrored images:** first500-027 and video frames from 028, 029, 062 show horizontally flipped lettering — a camera/optics setup artifact, not a coin feature. Any working image from these should be flipped back before comparison.
- **Drawn annotations are not coin features.** Red arrows, red lines, purple streaks, and circles appear on many photos (001, 002, 003, 005, 011, 016, 052, 082, 083, 089, 094, 097, 098, 101, 104, 108, 359, 360, the coin3 crack-map, the sail-rigging texture shot, and all four crack-template composites). Every anomaly call in this audit was based on the underlying pixels, not the markup.
- **Batch-09 report supersession.** Two examinations of files 278–321 initially disagreed on several per-file descriptions. The coordinator re-read the disputed files directly: the current `batch-09.md` is the accurate record (the prior draft misdescribed 278/279/294/295/296). The prior draft is preserved as `batch-09-PRIOR-DRAFT-backup.md` for transparency. Both versions agree on the bottom line (zero Phantom candidates).
- **Mirror tree.** An identical copy of the photo set exists under `goals/2026-mayflower-quarter-audit-phantom-of-larose/files/2026-mayflower-quarter-audit-phantom-of-larose/photos/` (verified file-identical). One worker read through the mirror; coverage is equivalent. If the two trees ever drift apart, the `your_files` tree is the audited original.
- **Personal material mixed in.** The 13 Louisiana OMV reinstatement-inquiry screenshots (first500-428–440) contain license/vehicle details. They were noted and excluded; their contents were not reproduced in any report.
- **Resolution limits.** Most full-coin shots are phone photos at modest resolution — die chips the size of the mosquito bite would not be visible even if present, so many marker verdicts on full-coin obverses are honestly UNCLEAR rather than proven absent. The negative findings are strongest where sharp macros exist (hand series, pinky macro 360, date-crack series, D-mint macros 359–367).

## 7. Recommendations (next steps, if David wants them)

1. **Re-photograph the leads at higher magnification:** the D10 A-1 elbow lump (456–459), the 147/148 rim (cud vs. damage), the 361/362/364/365 tilde marks, and the 064 video face crack. One sharp macro each would settle them.
2. **Photograph both faces of every candidate coin together.** Most files show only one face, which makes the all-five-marker rule untestable — a coin can never pass the rule from an obverse-only or reverse-only photo.
3. **Separate the working dies deliberately:** shoot the D-BowArc coins side by side to decide whether it is one die or one coin; do the same for the P-DateCrack cluster.
4. **File hygiene:** move the non-coin material (screenshots, lock screens, OMV documents, other denominations) out of the audit folder so future audits measure the coin set, not the camera roll.
5. **Do not present the crack templates as single-coin evidence.** Label them as composite checklists, which is what they are.
