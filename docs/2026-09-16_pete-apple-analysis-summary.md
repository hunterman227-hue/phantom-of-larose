## His conclusion
- The marks are **on the die** (he ruled out planchet-only issues after going down that dead end himself).
- Most likely explanation: **die flow waves** — die flow taking the form of waves terminating in nodes.
- "Mayflower is not showing spalling in any examples I have seen. It is all die flow."
- He is **not convinced die flow should be described as a progression** the way a die crack would be.

## What he ruled out
- **Spalling** — says the user is confusing spalling with die flow; his Sept 1 article on 2026-P
  Emerging Liberty dimes is about spalling and has nothing to do with Mayflower or over-tonnage.
- **Ghosting** — "Your coin does not have ghosting in the sense the word applies in numismatic descriptions."
- **Feeder fingers** — "No Feeder Fingers involved here... two totally separate events" from a die clash.
- **Die clash** — rejected the "triple die clash" reading of the three lines.
- **PIDT** (progressive indirect design transfer) — "I have already discussed with you on several
  occasions that your coin is NOT PIDT. I am done with that."
- **Over-tonnage** — "I don't know what it means and no evidence it exists."
- A die crack following the opposite-side design = "inconsequential and coincidental."
- The degree of finning = "inconsequential and need not be mentioned."

## Technical points he taught
- Misaligned die vs off-center strike: off-center is equally off on both sides; misaligned die is
  off on one side only, usually the hammer die. The obverse of the user's coin was struck by the hammer die.
- Mayflower quarters were most likely struck on horizontal-stroke Schuler presses (Schuler MRH 150).
- Reference site: https://www.error-ref.com/

## On value

  die design and how the design characteristics of the devices affect striking results."
- "I do not see $value for those coins."
